# Archive method: observation, source, interpretation

> Fictional demonstration. This method belongs to an invented archive.

A useful wiki page lets a reader distinguish what the record says from what someone thinks it means. Lantern Bay uses three simple layers.

## The three layers

1. **Observation or source:** the original note, sketch, or fictional interview fragment.
2. **Context:** where it came from, the conditions, and what is missing.
3. **Interpretation:** a claim that links records, with uncertainty stated explicitly.

## Link with a reason

A link should help a reader answer a question. [[night-sky-survey|night sky survey]] and [[navigation-history|navigation stories]] connect through horizon landmarks. [[salt-marsh|salt marsh listening station]] and [[oral-history|oral history collection]] connect through the inlet. [[harvest-calendar|harvest calendar]] makes their different kinds of timing visible.

## Small review checklist

- Can a reader identify the source type?
- Are fictional material and uncertainty labeled?
- Does the [[field-notebook|field notebook]] preserve the original question?
- Is there a useful route back to [[home|Lantern Bay field atlas]]?
